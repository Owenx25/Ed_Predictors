# Ed_Predictors

Standardized test performance varies greatly across U.S. states, but what are the potential factors that teachers and administrations can use to identify such trends? Publicly available data from the Civil Rights Data Collection, a biennial survey of public school data conducted by the Department of Education, and EdFacts, a public data initiative, both provide comprehensive data on the school and school-district level. This paper examines the relationship between key school characteristics such as student-teacher ratio, enrollment, chronic absences, full time equivalent of teachers, salary data and other potentially relevant characteristics of schools to predict standardized testing performance and determine feature importance for the 2017-2018 school year. Analysis through the use of decision trees and gradient boosting models, as well as an examination of the preprocessed data itself reveals interesting insights that show characteristics such as chronic absences, teacher salaries, and suspensions could potentially have a higher impact on standardized testing performance in elementary and high schools. With more information about what characteristics could impact such test scores, policymakers can allocate financial or personnel resources to schools that need it most while also gaining insight from those that are performing well.
